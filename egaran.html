Little Wishes for Little Treasures

by Egaran, Chelsea        |        July 24, 2025

     Long, sticky, sweaty, and dusty commutes with passengers who are fighting for the seats closest to the jeepney’s rear entrance as if it were the highest throne of the late Tudor monarchy, just so they will not have to keep passing other people’s fares to the driver. Sidewalks that are peppered with varying modes of transport, and pedestrian lanes that would make my aunties recite a whole prayer so that the next vehicle that would come, be compelled to at least slow down and not propel our beings to oblivion.

    In the city, we become seasoned warriors—guards and senses are always up and polished, it is a must to always know what, where, and how to do things, and to always act with purpose and precision.

    And so, the air buzzes a little with excitement when school breaks are just right around the corner because that means I will get to open the latch of our wooden gate, and be welcomed by the half a dozen dogs wagging their tails in our little hometown in Masinloc, Zambales.

    Long, cold, foggy morning walks through small roads with the occasional encounter of empty lots with massive strong trees sprinkled with little creatures singing on the branches that would make me feel the Hozier yell in Northern Attitude– where my heart feels like it is of an elephant’s and is pushing tears of appreciation for life at the corners of my eyes. Beaches and islands that do not need a three-month long planning just to go to. Sunsets that are orchestrated by fairies so that whoever looks be bewitched to never look away until the sun dipped at the salty ocean at Baywalk Municipality Park. The small red hill with the most refreshing view atop, and also perfect for beginner hikers, Mount Batolino. And amongst all, my favorite, for I believed when I witnessed it, could be a place that the Pemberley court be situated, is that vast and luscious green field in Lipay with a full view of the mountain at the distance, the soft ripple of the river just below the side of the land, the sharp prickly scent of grass, and on the far distance are trees bending down at each other building a canopy for a small entrance for other people in the area to pass through, though until now I still think it is a portal to another dimension.

    Every little, nature-bound, cost-free adventure that I have had within Masinloc feels like the tune created by the sequence of plucks in Would That I’s intro. And a testament that life is created like a lyrical composition of Hozier– with intellect and intention. And it is okay to just be, and wander, and feel your soul rather than the bustling world.

    It is in the undoneness of the urban persona that I hope that in the plans for advancement for our town may it not be spearheaded towards the typical approach of the action— developments of sky rise infrastructures at the expense of an indie mountain, or another seaside being packed with dolomite for a new mall to stake. I wish that it may not be with the passion to alter for mainstream entertainment so it suits the consumption of the general public.

    These tiny, genuine, and humble discoveries within tiny towns may not be up to par with the Mayon or Boracay, or are worlds apart with Bonifacio Global Center— it has a high chance of not being located by any foreign or local tourists at all, but it could be someone’s home— someone’s own little national treasure.
